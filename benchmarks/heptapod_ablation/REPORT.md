# Does heptapod raise the success rate? What the stored runs and new runs show (2026-10-06)

Files: `RETRO.md` and `retro.json` (generated by `retro.py`),
`HEPTAPOD_FIXES.md`, `DESIGN.md`, `results/`, `sm_override/`, `TRAIL.tsv`.
No 16-paper arm was run. New agent runs: one paper under three arms (smoke
test, $3.16 plus one referee call).

## Short answer

- **A loop with the chain in it raises the pass count. That part holds.**
  Same model, same 16 papers, one seed: 2 chain passes with no loop, 11 with
  the harness (exact McNemar p = 0.004). At an equal budget of 6 validate
  calls it is 8 against 2 (p = 0.031).
- **The 11 is too high.** Two of the 11 passes have no vertex for any new
  particle, and three have a physics-changing referee finding. Seven of 16
  pass the chain, have a clean referee and have every new particle in a
  vertex. The two no-tools passes are sound on the vertex test (their
  referee was not run).
- **"Heptapod, and not any loop" is weak.** No stored arm isolates it. The
  stored data say this much: a compile-only loop cannot see 8 of the 12
  failing first drafts; a loop that also tries the MadGraph import sees all
  of them in part, and misses the failing FeynRules check in 4. Of the 7
  sound passes, 3 needed no loop, 2 needed only the MadGraph error, and 2
  needed a FeynRules check. So the claim that is specific to heptapod's
  checks rests on two papers and one seed.
- **Half of the in-loop fixes came after a report section that heptapod
  does not have** (15 of 29: the located hermiticity, mass-matrix and
  MadGraph sections, and the lint). The other 14 came after information
  that heptapod-dev main also returns. This is what the agent had been told
  before a fix, not a measured effect.
- **What to change is clear, and three items are confirmed by runs.**
  `HEPTAPOD_FIXES.md` ranks them. Five of the nine papers that miss the
  endpoint trace to three defects that no tool names: a field with a partial
  index list, a class redeclared outside the class list, and new physics
  that never reaches the UFO.

## 1. Retrospective ablation

All counts are from `retro.py` on the stored files. One seed per arm.

### What the loop repaired

| item | passes at the first validate call | passes in the scored file |
|---|---|---|
| compile | 11 | 14 |
| hermiticity | 9 | 13 |
| kinetic_diagonal | 11 | 13 |
| mass_diagonal | 9 | 14 |
| mass_spectrum | 8 | 13 |
| MadGraph import | 3 | 13 |
| full chain | 3 | 11 |

15 runs made a first call (ALRM_general wrote no file). First-call stage:
compile 4, checks 4, MadGraph 4, pass 3. The no-tools arm's files: compile
11, mass_diagonal 5, import 4, full chain 2.

- How: the first call is `validation/call1/summary.json`; the scored file is
  `validation_hepval.json`.
- What it means: the first draft of the harness arm already has the protocol
  prompt, and in 8 of 15 runs a lint with findings before the first call. It
  passes mass_diagonal in 9 against the no-tools arm's 5. So the recipe and
  the pre-validation tools move the checks. They do not move the chain: 3
  against 2. The loop moves the chain, and the largest single repair is the
  MadGraph import (3 to 13).
- What can be wrong: the first draft is not a no-tools draft (different
  prompt, file tools, lint, massbasis), so "3 against 2" is not a controlled
  comparison of the prompt.

### What the agent had been told before each fix

29 item verdicts went from fail to pass between consecutive calls; 10 went
from pass to fail.

| item | fixed | located report (ALE) | lint rule cleared (ALE) | compile log | MadGraph's message | verdict only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| compile | 6 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 5 |
| hermiticity | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| mass_diagonal | 4 | 4 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| mass_spectrum | 4 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| MadGraph import | 11 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 0 |
| all | 29 | 15 | 2 | 1 | 7 | 6 |

15 fixes had an ALE-only source; 14 had only what heptapod-dev main returns.
`massbasis` was called between two validate calls before none of the fixes;
it was used before the first call in 4 runs. The referee ran in 11 runs.

- How: a fix is a change of an item's verdict between calls k and k+1. Its
  sources are what call k's report contained for that item (a located
  section), a lint rule of that item's stage that was present at k and gone
  at k+1, and the kind of compile or MadGraph failure.
- What can be wrong: "came after" is not "was caused by". A report section
  counts as located when it exists, not when it is right (the 331 MadGraph
  section pointed at the wrong lines once). A fix of an item hidden by a
  compile failure is not counted. Lint findings before the first call have
  no "before" state, so the lint's main use is not in this table.

  from one cut at 6. In-loop verdicts come from the diagnostic driver; they
  agreed with the score in all 10 runs that can be compared.

### Lint alone on the no-tools files

15 files, 13 fail. The lint names at least one failing item in 6, every
failing item in 4 (ALRM_general, B-L-SM, NJLComposite, Wprime), and nothing
in 4 (EffLRSM, HeavyN, Monotops, Top-Philic-Zprime). Per item: compile 2 of
4, hermiticity 0 of 4, mass_diagonal 2 of 6, mass_spectrum 2 of 7, import 2
of 7. One of the two passing files (pNG) has an error-level finding.

- What it means: at most 4 of the 13 failures are of a kind the lint could
  have turned, and naming is not fixing.
- What can be wrong: several rules were written from files of these arms, so
  the hit rate is in-sample.

## 2. Heptapod's own bottlenecks

`HEPTAPOD_FIXES.md` has the list with evidence, flips and effort. In order:

1. A gate for new particles that have no vertex (upstream has the data in
   `vertex_inventory` and fails nothing).
2. An index-count check for fermion fields (`l[ff]` puts `ff` in the spin
   slot). 35 of 102 stored snapshots have it; 32 of those fail hermiticity
   or lose their new vertices.
3. The SM-override text of the skill is wrong for the driver's load order,
   and a class redeclared outside the class list is ignored without a word.
4. No tool says which block fails to compile.
5. Mass rows and gauge: upstream returns the rows and takes the gauge; the
   ALE wrapper drops both.
6. Static checks for duplicate parameter names and couplings of order '1'.
7. The ALE located reports (88 driver lines), to move upstream.
8. The MadGraph back-trace, the lint and `massbasis`, to move upstream.

On two points the ALE wrapper shows the agent less than heptapod would:
the mass-spectrum rows and the new-physics vertex count.

## 3. The clean ablation

`DESIGN.md`. Arms, endpoint, test, power, cost and commands are fixed there.

- Recommended: design A, repair from the same 18 stored first drafts, arms
  `import`, `heptapod`, `diag`, 2 seeds: 108 runs, about $295, about 26
  kernel hours. Power 0.89 for `heptapod` against `import` if check feedback
  repairs 45% and generic feedback 10%; 0.45 if it repairs 30%.
- Design B, end to end on 11 papers, has four papers that can differ between
  arms. `full` against `compile` needs 5 seeds for a power of 0.83 under the
  central assumption (about $410); `heptapod` against `compile` does not
  reach 0.8 at 6 seeds.
- Design C, hepbench `core_only` against `tools_model`, is the only test of
  the released toolkit. It cannot run here: `toolbench` is not installed and
  the remote needs a login.
- Neither A nor B generalises beyond these papers. That needs more papers.

## What changed on this machine

- `sympy` 1.14.0 was installed into `~/hep-tools/env` (`massbasis` needs it).
- New runs are under `benchmarks/heptapod_ablation/runs/` (git-ignored).
  Nothing was written to the stored runs tree, heptapod-dev or hepbench-dev.
- Compile limit. On this machine the compile takes 1.5 times as long as on the Mac (median of 15 models, range 1.25 to 1.56). 331 needs 1198 s here (805 s on the Mac), so the default limit of 900 s turns its stored `compiled` into a timeout. With `HEPVAL_COMPILE_TIMEOUT=1350` (900 x 1.5) the limit means here what 900 s meant on the Mac. Any run or score that includes 331 must set it; the papers of designs A and B compile in 253 s or less.
